FAQ

Questions, answered

Is Calls really free?

Yes. There's no subscription, no trial timer, and no features locked behind a paywall. You download it, sign in, and call people.

Which platforms are supported?

Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit), and Linux (.deb for Debian/Ubuntu, .rpm for Fedora/RHEL) — Linux support is new, so it's marked beta while it settles. macOS is planned — the app's core is already cross-platform, so it's a matter of packaging, not a rewrite.

Windows warned me about an unrecognized app. Why?

That's Microsoft SmartScreen being cautious about new publishers. The installer isn't code-signed with a paid certificate yet, so Windows shows a generic warning for it. Click "More info" → "Run anyway" to proceed. Once code-signed, the warning disappears.

Code signing is on our roadmap; once in place, the warning disappears.

Do I need an account?

Yes — a lightweight one. An account is what lets friends find you by name and call you directly instead of passing meeting IDs around every time.

How do updates work?

On Windows, the app checks for new versions in the background, downloads only the files that changed, and verifies a cryptographic signature on every update before applying it — restart once and you're done. On Linux, updates ship through a signed APT/YUM repository instead: the app tells you one is available, and your package manager installs it (one click from inside the app if it can ask for your password, otherwise a regular apt upgrade/dnf upgrade). See what's changed in the changelog.

What data does Calls collect?

As little as it can get away with: your account details and what's needed to connect calls. Calls are never recorded, and crash reports are strictly opt-in and off by default. The full details are in the privacy policy.
